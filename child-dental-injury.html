Visit guide / Child dental injury

Parent checklist

A child chipped, loosened or knocked out a tooth

Start by checking the child, controlling bleeding and determining whether the injured tooth is a baby tooth or a permanent tooth.

Check the whole child first

Look for loss of consciousness, vomiting, confusion, neck pain, severe facial injury or difficulty breathing. Those concerns require medical evaluation. For a mouth injury, have the child sit upright, gently rinse or wipe away blood, and apply firm pressure with clean gauze to a bleeding area.

Baby tooth or permanent tooth?

Do not place a knocked-out baby tooth back into the socket because of the risk to the developing permanent tooth. A knocked-out permanent tooth is time-sensitive: hold it by the crown, avoid touching or scrubbing the root, keep it moist and contact a dentist immediately. If you are unsure which kind of tooth it is, call and describe the child’s age and tooth location.

Chipped or displaced tooth

Save fragments in a clean container. A small chip and a deep fracture can look similar to a parent, but exposure of deeper tooth layers may cause sensitivity or pain. If a tooth is pushed inward, outward or sideways, do not force it back; arrange prompt dental evaluation.

Lip, tongue and cheek injuries

Clean gently with water and use a cold compress outside the mouth for swelling. Seek emergency care for a deep wound, embedded object, uncontrolled bleeding or serious facial trauma.

Call promptly after a permanent tooth is knocked out, a tooth is displaced, a fracture is painful, bleeding will not stop, or swelling is increasing.

Prepare for the visit

  • Time and description of the injury
  • Whether the child lost consciousness
  • All tooth fragments or the recovered tooth
  • Medication list, allergies and health conditions
  • Photo of the injury if it is safe to take one
