Urgent dental problem checklist

You do not need to know the diagnosis. A few specific observations help the dental office understand urgency and reserve the right type of visit.

Describe what changed

  • Where is the pain—upper or lower, left or right, one tooth or a larger area?
  • When did it start, and is it constant, throbbing or only triggered by biting, cold or heat?
  • Is there swelling inside the mouth, in the cheek, beneath the jaw or near the eye?
  • Did a tooth, filling, crown, denture or other restoration break or fall out?
  • Was there an injury? If so, when did it happen and was consciousness affected?
  • Is there fever, a bad taste, drainage, bleeding or difficulty opening the mouth?

Protect broken or lost dental work

Keep a crown, denture fragment or piece of tooth in a clean container and bring it to the visit. Do not use household glue. If a permanent tooth is knocked out, handle it by the crown rather than the root, keep it moist and seek dental care immediately.

Call emergency medical services for difficulty breathing or swallowing, major facial trauma, rapidly spreading swelling, or bleeding that will not stop.

What happens next?

An urgent dental evaluation may include an examination and X-rays. Treatment depends on the diagnosis, the condition of the tooth and surrounding tissues, medical history, timing and available appointment resources.
