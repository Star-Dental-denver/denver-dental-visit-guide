Visit guide / Broken tooth or lost crown

Protect the area

A tooth broke or a crown came off—what should you do?

Save anything that came out, avoid household repairs and describe the condition of the tooth and restoration when you call.

First, identify what came out

A smooth hollow cap may be a crown. A smaller piece may be tooth structure or filling material. Do not worry if you cannot tell—place every recovered piece in a clean container and bring it. A clear photo may help the office understand what happened, but it cannot replace examination.

Temporary crown

If a temporary crown is intact, a dentist may be able to evaluate whether it can be repositioned. If it is cracked, distorted or missing, making a replacement may be more complicated—especially at a different office that does not have the original tooth form, impression or digital design. The prepared tooth may feel sensitive because the temporary restoration normally helps cover and protect it.

Permanent crown or filling

A permanent crown may come off because cement failed, decay developed, or the supporting tooth fractured. It should not simply be glued back without checking the tooth and fit. A lost filling can leave edges that trap food or irritate the tongue; avoid chewing hard foods on that side.

Do
  • Keep the piece in a clean container
  • Rinse gently if debris is present
  • Chew on the opposite side
  • Call promptly if pain or sensitivity develops
Do not
  • Use superglue or household adhesive
  • Force a crown that does not seat easily
  • File or reshape the tooth yourself
  • Ignore swelling, fever or worsening pain

What information should you share?

Tell the office whether the restoration was temporary or permanent, when it was placed, whether it is intact, whether part of the tooth broke with it, and whether the tooth is sensitive to air, temperature or biting. Mention swelling, trauma or a recent root canal.

Insurance and another dental office’s temporary

A temporary crown is commonly part of the overall crown procedure. If another office placed it, a different dentist’s examination or re-temporization may be treated as a separate service by the plan, and the patient may have an out-of-pocket cost. Coverage depends on the specific plan and clinical situation, so ask the office to review available information.
